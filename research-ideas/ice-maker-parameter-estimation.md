# Parameter estimation for the R600a ice maker

## What the temperature data can constrain

The three relevant fidelity levels serve different questions. A lumped thermal model can test whether water temperature, ambient temperature and thermal lag explain the measured trends. A pressure–enthalpy cycle can test phase and pressure scenarios. A dynamic inventory model can estimate charge and its distribution once there is enough information to constrain volume, pressure and liquid fraction. The last two levels need independent physical inputs beyond the present logs.

- In a lumped energy balance, $C\,\dot T=UA_w(T_w-T)+UA_a(T_a-T)+\dot Q_{\rm ref}$. With temperature histories and an unknown refrigerant duty, a common scaling of $C$, the conductances and duty leaves the temperature prediction invariant. Fit effective time constants or conductance-to-capacitance ratios when the data excite those terms independently. The fitted capacitance would include metal, nearby water and sensor response as well as refrigerant. It cannot be converted to charge without separating those contributions.
- The measured water is the evaporator heat source. A colder refrigerant-side surface is consistent with heat entering from water. Ambient air can cool a suction line if the line temperature exceeds the local air temperature and can warm it in the reverse case. The suction temperature alone cannot establish the evaporator heat duty or a low UA. Separate evaporation from downstream heat exchange with a refrigerant temperature at the evaporator exit and a water-side heat balance. Use flow and inlet/outlet temperatures for a flowing water stream, or known water mass, heater input and a heat-loss model for a batch.
- For the refrigerant cycle, use common R600a PH property arguments at compressor inlet/outlet, condenser exit and capillary exit. A starting capillary model is isenthalpic and needs a separate flow relation or specified mass flow. Before treating the downstream capillary measurement as saturation temperature, check whether its location plausibly observes two-phase refrigerant and account for possible pressure drop, line exchange and sensor offset. A pre-capillary liquid-line reading likewise differs from the condensing temperature when subcooled. The discharge line reading can include superheat. Model pipe-wall sensors with explicit bias or lag only when independently constrained.
- Vibration is consistent with slugging, and liquid return can also depend on flooding, migration and accumulated liquid. The saturation pressure at the measured suction temperature is a useful **equilibrium wet-suction scenario**, conditional on the sensor measuring the equilibrated refrigerant at the event. Droplets or a liquid slug can coexist transiently with superheated bulk vapour. Keep the vibration report as a regime flag and compare dry-suction, wet-equilibrium and sensor-bias scenarios. [Copeland liquid refrigerant control bulletin, AE22-1182](https://webapps.copeland.com/online-product-information/Publication/LaunchPDF?Index=AEB&PDF=1182)

### Property and inventory calculations

The NIST R600a saturation correlation over 261.31–408.12 K is $P_{\rm sat}[\mathrm{bar\ abs}]=10^{4.3281-1132.108/(T[\mathrm{K}]+0.918)}$. For the detailed model, Bücker and Wagner give reference equations for both butane isomers. CoolProp documents the R600a implementation under `IsoButane`. [NIST Chemistry WebBook](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C75285&Mask=207F&Plot=on&Type=ANTOINE&Units=SI), [Bücker and Wagner](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1901687), [CoolProp R600a properties](https://coolprop.org/fluid_properties/fluids/IsoButane.html)

Local check with IDAES 2.8.0 and the installed Ahuora property data. Select `i-butane` and mass basis in `HelmholtzParameterBlock` while the IDAES data path points to the Ahuora `helmholtz/parameters` directory. Evaluate `p_sat(T=...)`, `1/v_liq_sat(T=...)` and `1/v_vap_sat(T=...)` from `HelmholtzThermoExpressions` inside that context. The repository's existing [Helmholtz provider](../src/multifidelity/model_language/standard/thermo.py) switches directories for both `n-butane` and `i-butane`. A reusable symbolic isobutane model can use the common PH output contract, omitting viscosity where the data lack transport correlations. The n-butane steam-cycle model uses the other isomer.

For a volume $V$ with vapour void fraction $\alpha$, $m=V[(1-\alpha)\rho_l+\alpha\rho_v]$. Equivalently, for equilibrium mass quality $x$, $m=V/[(1-x)/\rho_l+x/\rho_v]$. A 100 mL saturated R600a volume at 20 °C holds 0.79 g as vapour, 6.28 g at 10% liquid by volume, or 55.69 g filled with liquid. The factor of about 70 between the end points persists even when the saturation temperature is known. The example volume is a normalization. Estimate geometric volumes from component dimensions and combine them with phase scenarios to get conditional component masses. For a sealed system, the total charge is conserved while its distribution varies, including any compressor oil inventory the chosen model accounts for. A factory nameplate charge can serve as a prior if the loop has remained sealed.

For a dynamic pressure–enthalpy inventory, $M_j=V_j\rho_j$, $U_j=M_j h_j-P_j V_j$, $\dot M_j=\dot m_{in,j}-\dot m_{out,j}$, and $\dot U_j=\dot m_{in,j}h_{in,j}-\dot m_{out,j}h_{out,j}+\dot Q_j$ in the exchanger and pipe volumes. The [pipe holdup](../src/multifidelity/model_language/models/pipe_holdup.json) already uses these balances for an adiabatic, perfectly mixed pipe whose pressure follows its inlet. For a charge-determining cycle, the high- and low-side pressures need closure through their own inventory states, heat and flow balances. Include volumes on both sides, and add independent exchanger wall capacity before attempting to explain the observed lag by refrigerant inventory.

The thermodynamic design point for atmospheric steam is around 100 °C at the sink. A model imposing a 5 K condenser approach gives about 21.8 bar absolute refrigerant saturation pressure, compared with about 19.9 bar at zero approach. Those values describe R600a saturation states. Machine operating limits and sustained steam duty require a compressor envelope and heat-rejection evidence. [NIST water phase-change data](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C7732185&Mask=4)

## IDAES and Pyomo workflow

The IDAES parameter-estimation tutorial applies Pyomo's `parmest` to both a property state block and a unit model. The economizer example builds and initializes a model for each reconciled operating case, then estimates common equipment parameters across cases. These examples illustrate the architecture. The repository pins Pyomo 6.9.2, whose labeled `Experiment` interface should guide an implementation. A local smoke check with that version and IPOPT recovered a shared bounded parameter of 3 across two synthetic experiments from starting value 1. [IDAES state-block estimation](https://idaes.github.io/examples-pse/latest/Tutorials/Advanced/ParamEst/parameter_estimation_NRTL_using_state_block_solution_doc.html), [IDAES unit-model estimation](https://idaes.github.io/examples-pse/latest/Tutorials/Advanced/ParamEst/parameter_estimation_NRTL_using_unit_model_solution_doc.html), [IDAES economizer example](https://idaes.github.io/examples-pse/latest/Examples/Advanced/DataRecon/econ_parmest_doc.html), [Pyomo 6.9.2 Parmest driver](https://pyomo.readthedocs.io/en/6.9.2/explanation/analysis/parmest/driver.html)

1. Keep `InformationDocument` as evidence. Each temperature series maps to a public variable path with an observation ID, unit, uncertainty where known and trust rank. Convert temperatures from °C to K at ingestion, preserving the original channel and timestamps in the data provenance. Temperature differences in °C and K have the same magnitude. Trust rank orders evidence for specification and should be kept distinct from statistical weight.
2. Keep `SpecificationDocument` for hard inputs and initial conditions. Add a separate calibration plan at the solve layer, above the model language. It should select an implementation, a dataset window and time grid per experiment, the hard specification, soft observations, and bounded parameter paths with starting values or priors. The existing [specification relaxation](../src/multifidelity/model_language/reconciliation.py) shows how to clone a model, resolve indexed paths and report residuals. Its current entry point requires prior fixed observations, whereas parameter estimation can attach residuals directly to free model outputs. Reuse the mapping and unit conversion where practical.
3. Build a square forward problem with candidate parameters fixed, diagnose structural closure, initialize it and solve it first. For dynamics, discretize the whole continuous trajectory and map residuals to actual observation points. Intermediate grid points used by collocation are model states and need no fabricated measurements. Fit shared physical parameters and experiment-specific initial states or nuisance terms. For a single trajectory, a Pyomo objective and IPOPT are sufficient. For several comparable runs, `Experiment.get_labeled_model()` can return a fresh initialized model with `experiment_outputs[VarData] = target` and `unknown_parameters[parameter_var] = parameter_var`. Parmest links the labeled parameters in its extensive form. Individual rows of a dynamic run share state and should stay together. [Pyomo DAE](https://pyomo.readthedocs.io/en/6.9.2/explanation/modeling/dae.html), [Pyomo 6.9.2 Parmest driver](https://pyomo.readthedocs.io/en/6.9.2/explanation/analysis/parmest/driver.html)
4. Start with a weighted objective $\sum[(T_{\rm pred}-T_{\rm obs})/\sigma_T]^2$ when the chosen errors can be approximated as independent. If sensor precision is unknown, use documented modeling weights and report their sensitivity. Preserve consecutive time blocks for holdout validation and inspect serial residuals. The pinned Parmest covariance approximation assumes independent, identically distributed errors and unweighted SSE, so a dense, autocorrelated temperature trace does not justify its nominal interval. Use multi-start fits, scaled sensitivity rank and profile objectives for charge, UA and sensor lag. If profiles remain flat or terminate at imposed bounds, report conditional scenarios and identifiable parameter combinations. [Pyomo 6.9.2 Parmest overview](https://pyomo.readthedocs.io/en/6.9.2/explanation/analysis/parmest/overview.html), [Pyomo 6.9.2 covariance assumptions](https://pyomo.readthedocs.io/en/6.9.2/explanation/analysis/parmest/covariance.html)
5. Export calibration results separately with implementation selection, source and fit-window identifiers, solver status, physical residuals, estimates and bounds, channel residuals, and validation metrics. Use a reviewed calibration to produce new parameter information records and forward-run them through the same specification and results interfaces. Across fidelities, transfer quantities with shared physical meaning, such as measured geometry and charge, and compare held-out observations. An effective thermal time constant should seed a detailed fit only through its model relationship to the detailed parameters.

The first discriminating measurements are paired suction and discharge pressures with compressor status, the exact vibration timestamp, nominal charge and approximate internal volumes, and water-side heat balance data. Low-side pressure together with suction temperature gives superheat during ordinary operation. Pressure and temperature together still leave equilibrium two-phase quality free. Volume and independent energy or inventory information are needed to estimate the liquid distribution.
